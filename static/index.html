Welcome

This is a custom planning tool built for the couple's wedding, like a private version of The Knot. It runs on the couple's own server, not a third-party service. Feel free to click around. Nothing here sends an email, texts a guest, or deletes data just from looking at it. The few actions that do something real, like sending an invite or syncing RSVPs, are called out below and always need a deliberate click on a clearly labeled button.

Dashboard

A quick-glance summary: the countdown to the wedding, overall planning progress (based on the Timeline tab's checklist), what's coming up next, and anything that's waiting on a decision or a reply from someone.

Venues

Once a venue is Booked this tab becomes that venue's page: contract, payments, what's coming up, documents, contact log and tour notes. The other venues fold up under "Show other venues", and the ranking, comparing and adding tools go away. Change the venue's status from Booked and the full list comes back.

Adding a venue: fill in the form by hand, or paste the venue's website link and hit "Fetch & review." A local AI model reads the page and fills in what it can find: name, location, price, capacity, services. Always double-check what it filled in, especially the location, before saving.

Drive time: shown on each venue's card. It's calculated once, not kept automatically up to date. If you edit a venue's address, or a drive time looks wrong, click "Recalculate" on that card.

Ranking & comparing: rate venues with the star ranking, then select a few and hit "Compare" for a side-by-side table. "Summarize comparison" asks the local AI model for a quick pros/cons summary of the venues you picked. It's a writing aid, not a decision-maker, so give it a read before trusting it.

Fill in a venue's Contact email so "Sync venue emails" can match its mail to it. Many venues use a booking platform (TripleSeat, HoneyBook, etc.), and the sync already handles the two ways those tend to break a plain address match: it checks a message's Reply-To in addition to who it's visibly from (catches TripleSeat's shared "noreply@" auto-replies, whose Reply-To is the venue's real address), and it also matches on the sender's exact display name (catches HoneyBook, whose address rotates per conversation but whose display name is reliably the venue's own name). If a venue's activity still isn't showing up, add whatever address it actually uses under "Other addresses they email from" — including a "@domain.com" wildcard if it's their own real domain.

Tour notes is a running checklist for site visits, in each venue's Details. Click "Load standard questions" for a starting list of common things to ask/check on a tour (hours, load-in, restrictions, equipment, pricing breakdown, contract terms, and more), or add your own. Each answer saves automatically when you click away from it — no separate save button.

Vendors

Same idea as Venues, for caterers, photographers, florists, and other vendors. Paste a link to auto-fill, or enter by hand. Each vendor also has a contact log for jotting down when you reached out and what was said, handy once you're juggling several conversations.

Site review reads a vendor's own website for you. Use Review site in a vendor's Details, or Compare sites to review a whole category and see packages, prices and details side by side. It reads up to 8 pages per site, slowly and politely, so allow a few minutes per vendor. Prices come from a plain text search, so they're shown with the words around them. Check what they're for, because a site's lowest price may not be a wedding price. If a site has price lists for different years, each is labeled and the comparison leads with your wedding year. Everything the AI adds has to match the page word for word, and anything not on the site shows as "Not listed", which makes a handy list of questions to ask. Some sites refuse automated reading; for those, copy the page text from your browser and use Paste page text. Nothing is saved to the vendor until you press Save.

If a vendor has a physical shop you might visit (a florist, a bakery, a bridal shop), fill in Address and Hours so you know when they're open. If someone from a vendor is staying for the reception and needs a seat, usually a photographer, videographer, DJ/band, or officiant, check "Needs a seat at the reception" on their form. That's what makes them show up on the Seating tab. It's checked by default for those categories, but you can change it per vendor.

Sync vendor emails (also on the Venues tab as "Sync venue emails" — either button runs the same check) runs by itself every 15 minutes, and the button checks right away. It reads recent messages from the shared mailbox and matches them to vendors and venues by email address, Reply-To, or exact sender display name (see Venues above for what that last one is for — same platforms, same fix, no separate setup for Vendors). It also keeps a thread together and notices a vendor's or venue's name in the subject or body. Real-looking mail from anyone else appears on the Dashboard under "Emails to place," where you pick who it belongs to; newsletters and login codes are skipped. When it finds a match, the local AI model suggests a status update and/or a proposed appointment, shown under that card's Details as "Recent email activity." Nothing changes on its own — accept a suggestion to actually apply it, or dismiss it. Read-only against the mailbox: this never sends anything.

Each vendor and venue card also shows a second badge next to its status — "Waiting on vendor," "Needs your response," or "In progress." This is separate from Status (which tracks the overall funnel — booked, declined, etc.) and answers "whose turn is it right now." It updates itself from synced email automatically (whoever sent the last message, that's who the other side is waiting on), and you can also set it by hand when logging a phone/in-person contact — whichever happened more recently wins, so there's nothing to keep in sync manually.

Guests & RSVPs

Guests are grouped into parties (a household or invite group). Each party can have multiple people listed inside it, useful for tracking meal preferences or a plus-one separately from the person who RSVP'd.

Sync RSVPs pulls the latest responses from the wedding website and updates each party's status here (yes / no / not yet) to match. It's one-way and read-only against the website. It never changes anything there, and it's always safe to click.

Push Invites generates a personal, pre-filled RSVP link for every party that doesn't already have one. It doesn't send anything by itself, it just creates the links. After pushing, each party gets three actions: Copy invite link (grab the URL to paste anywhere), Send invite (emails it to the party directly from here), and QR code (a scannable code for a paper invite that opens the same pre-filled link).

There's no "send to everyone" button on purpose. Invites go out one party (or a deliberate batch) at a time, so nothing goes out by accident. "Download CSV" exports the full guest list, including each party's invite link and QR code image link, handy for mail-merging paper invites.

Wedding Party

Anyone given a wedding party role on the Guests tab appears here, grouped by the side they stand on. To add someone, click Edit on their party in Guests and fill in Wedding party role and Standing side next to their name. Both are set per person, so a couple can stand on opposite sides, and neither one changes the party's side in the guest list.

The role list also has family roles, like Mother of the Bride or Grandfather of the Groom. They show in their own Bride's family and Groom's family cards, so they don't need a standing side. For a role that isn't listed, pick Other and write it the same way, like "Stepmother of the Bride", and it goes in the right card too.

They're still regular guests, so headcount, RSVPs, invites, and seating work the same. Their role also shows in the Seating picker, which helps when planning a head table, and in the CSV download.

Tools

A place for useful websites and apps that aren't businesses you're hiring, like a color palette maker. Each one is just a name, a link, and notes, with no status or follow-ups to keep up with.

Seating

Set up tables and assign parties to them. A party can be moved between tables at any time. Nothing about seating affects RSVP status or invites.

Only guests and vendors marked "Needs a seat at the reception" (see Vendors above) show up as assignable here. Most vendors deliver, set up, and leave, so they're left out on purpose.

Timeline

The engagement-to-wedding-day checklist. This drives the "Planning progress" bar on the Dashboard, and it's organized into phases so it's clear what needs attention now versus later. Any task can have a due date, and past-due ones are marked Overdue.

Click the circle next to a task, or its title, to move it through Not started, In progress and Done. "Book..." tasks also follow your vendors: logging a contact with a photographer moves "Book photographer/videographer" to In progress, and shows how many have been contacted. When a vendor is marked Booked, the task offers a "Mark done" button rather than closing itself. Setting a status by hand wins over the vendors until you click "Follow vendors again". Logging a contact pre-fills a follow-up 3 days out; clear the date if you don't need one.

Tasks can also come from your contracts. On the Budget tab's Documents card, Review requirements lists things a document says you need to do, like confirming a rehearsal date, each with the exact wording it came from. Deadlines like "60 days prior to event" are worked out from your wedding date. Passages under "Also worth checking" come from a text search rather than the AI and start unticked. Tick the ones you want and they're added to the phase their date falls in, linked back to the vendor and the document. A deadline is set once, so if the wedding date changes, check any dates that came from a contract.

Schedule

Day-of and lead-up appointments: venue tours, vendor meetings, fittings. Separate from the bigger-picture Timeline checklist.

Budget

Set an overall budget, then log individual items by category. Each item can have an estimated cost and, once something is signed, a contracted amount. The progress bar at the top shows what you've actually paid against the budget.

Click Payments on an item to add its payment schedule (deposit, installments, final balance) and record payments as you make them. "Paid" is always added up from those recorded payments, and each scheduled payment's status (Paid, Due soon, Overdue, and so on) updates by itself. A payment can cover part of a scheduled amount, or sit outside the schedule altogether, like a tip.

Documents at the bottom of the Budget tab holds contracts, invoices, and receipts, as PDFs or photos up to 25 MB. Link one to a venue or vendor and it also shows in their Details, or to a budget item and it shows under that item's Payments. Deleting a venue, vendor, or budget item keeps its documents, so a signed contract can't disappear by accident. Only Remove on the document itself deletes the file.

Each upload is read in the background, which can take a few minutes for photos. When it says Ready to review, click Review to check what was found: the vendor, the contract total, scheduled payments, and any payment a receipt confirms. Fix anything that's wrong, untick what you don't want, and click Add to budget. Sections marked "Read by AI" are the ones to check most carefully. Under each document, code also checks that the totals, tax, and payment schedule add up, that any event date matches your wedding date, and that your guest list fits any guest limits in the document. Lines and payments already in the budget are skipped, so nothing counts twice. Scanned PDFs and HEIC photos can't be read yet, so those open as a blank form to fill in yourself.

You'll see a warning if a schedule doesn't add up to the contracted amount or payments go over it, but nothing stops you saving. Removing a scheduled payment keeps any payments already recorded against it.
